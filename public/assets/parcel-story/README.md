# Parcel story — every image of the truck story, in story order

This is the scroll story in the services section: the Connext truck is already standing at the first stop
with the customer waiting at a boutique; the partner (who is also the driver) leaves his seat, comes out from
behind the truck's rear, takes **1** parcel and pushes it into the truck, goes back behind it to his seat, the truck
drives on, he does the same with **3** corporate boxes at a company's office entrance, then the camera flips overhead
and the truck drives the road. All of its pictures live here, numbered in the order they appear.

```
parcel-story/
├─ 1-street/            the street: two pickups in front, a row of buildings and footpath life behind
│  ├─ people/             partner-walk, -receive, -carry-1, -place, -carry-3 (corporate boxes), -driving (seated, hands on the wheel),
│  │                      customer-offer, customer-stand (pickup 1), seller-corporate (pickup 2 executive)
│  ├─ places/             shop-boutique (pickup 1), office-corporate (pickup 2) — both modern
│  ├─ parcels/            parcel-corp1…corp5 — corporate boxes (corp1–3 are used at pickup 2)
│  ├─ backdrop/           bld-elec, -cafe, -it, -retail, -med, -fin — medium-rise shops and company buildings with furnished,
│  │                      lit interiors and people inside (Chennai-style, modern)
│  ├─ life/               life-p01…p12 (pedestrians), life-moto, -scooter, -bicycle, -cart (fruit vendor), life-cafe-couple / -reader / -menu
│  └─ manifest.json       pixel size + torso anchor of each cut-out; `m` = real height in metres for the life/ props
├─ 2-truck-side/        the truck seen from the side
│  ├─ 01-truck-complete     the whole truck, Connext logo on the box
│  └─ 02-wheel-front / 03-wheel-rear   the wheels, spun by the page on top of the truck
├─ 3-truck-flip/        000.avif … 044.avif — the 45 frames of the side → overhead flip
├─ 4-truck-top/         01-truck-top — the truck from above, the full Connext logo on its roof
└─ 5-mobile/            truck-top — the vertical top-down truck on phones
```

All files are `.avif` with transparency, except `manifest.json`.

## Where each part comes from

| Folder | Made with | To change it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `1-street/` | Magnific image generation (Nano Banana Pro), then background removal | Generate a replacement, cut it out, save it under the **same file name**. Keep a person's feet at the bottom edge of the image. If the size changes, update its entry in `manifest.json` (`w`, `h`, `cx` = torso position as a fraction of the width). |
| `2-truck-side/`, `3-truck-flip/`, `4-truck-top/`, `5-mobile/` | One 3D truck, rendered by `tools/truck-render/` | Change the truck in `tools/truck-render/build.html` (the logo on the box, its size, the truck model), render, then run `python3 tools/truck-render/export.py`. It rewrites all four folders together, so they stay in step. Do not edit these files by hand. |

## Where the page uses them

- The HTML is generated by `python3 scripts/build-sections.py` (it reads this folder, including
  `manifest.json`). Change a file name or add a figure there, then re-run it.
- **Pacing — one number.** How much scroll the pickup story takes is `--story` (in vh, on `.home-service` in
  `src/styles/service.css`; 250 now) on desktop and `--story-mb` (in svh, in `service-mobile.css`; 290 now) on phones.
  Lower = faster. The pinned stage, the drive after it and the page length all follow that number.
- **Motion** (all in `src/sections/street-story.js`, one scrubbed timeline 3.2 units long): every move eases in and out; steps bob and
  sway; the truck lifts its nose pulling away, bounces over the road and dips braking; the wheels turn the distance the street slides;
  the three street layers blur with speed and wind streaks cross the scene; chips slide in, the parcel counter rolls like an odometer,
  "+1 parcel" / "+3 parcels" pop over the truck and the route pill fills; the skyline rises as the stage scrolls in; standing figures
  breathe while the section is on screen. Contact shadows sit under every figure and prop (service.css). Sprites are pre-decoded so
  nothing hitches the first time it appears. `prefers-reduced-motion` keeps the choreography but drops blur, streaks, bounce and idle loops.
- **Responsive — every device.** Three layouts (≥ 992 px laptop/desktop, 768–991 px tablet, ≤ 767 px phone) plus the shapes in
  between, all checked from 360 × 740 phones to 3440 × 1440 ultra-wide, portrait and landscape:
  - the street (backdrop of buildings) is fitted to the screen's shape by `fitStreet()` in `street-story.js` — smaller on wide, short
    screens so the roofs stay on screen, bigger on tall tablets and phones so the sky is used; three copies of the street keep it
    continuous at any size (`--far-scale`, `.is-far` in service.css);
  - on a phone, one metre (`--mm`, service-mobile.css) follows `min(7.2vw, 11.5dvh)`, so a phone turned on its side gets a smaller
    street (no headline, small readouts) instead of a cropped one; heights use `dvh`, so the browser's address bar never leaves a gap;
  - short screens get more scroll (`--story` rises below 640 px and 480 px of height) so the story is never over in a flick;
  - the readouts respect notches and rounded corners (`env(safe-area-inset-*)`);
  - when the viewport really changes (window resize, a tablet turned on its side, a foldable opening) `service.js` tears the section
    down and builds it again for the new size and breakpoint — no reload needed; a phone's address bar sliding away does not count.
- Scroll timing: `src/sections/street-story.js`. Layout and metre-based positions: `src/styles/service.css`
  (desktop) and `src/styles/service-mobile.css`.
- Everything in `1-street/` is placed in **metres from the truck's rear edge**, so a 1.78 m partner is
  the right size next to the truck whatever the screen.
- The street behind is built in `street_inner()` in the generator, as three layers that slide at different speeds
  (so it has depth): `is-far` — the six `backdrop/` buildings standing side by side on a footpath
  (`.home-service-footpath`, 0.5 m high), 0.6×; `is-mid` — the footpath life from `life/` (cafe terrace, pedestrians,
  fruit cart, parked bikes), 0.7×; `is-near` — the two pickups, 1×. The building heights (7.6–8.8 m, three to five
  floors) and every position are in that one function. To change the look, replace a file under the same name
  (front-on, ground line at the bottom edge) and update its `w`/`h` in `manifest.json`.
  The earlier weathered shop and warehouse, the seller and the e-commerce parcels are in `_backup-semi-truck/old-street/`.
- The partner is **not** in the street layer: he lives on the truck's own layers (`crew()` in the generator).
  While he drives, `partner-driving` (waist up, hands on the wheel) is drawn *under* the truck picture, whose cab
  window is see-through, so only the window shows him. When he gets out, the poses appear at the truck's rear and
  that layer is cut off at the rear edge, so he comes out from behind the truck and goes back behind it; he never
  walks across the truck's side. If you replace `partner-driving`, keep it waist-up (no legs) and re-place it
  with the `spr('seat', …)` line in `crew()` (plate pixels ÷ 200 = metres).

Older versions of all of this (semi-trailer truck, container yard and crane, earlier trucks) are in `_backup-semi-truck/`.
